A central bank (or reserve bank) manages a state's currency, money supply, and interest rates. It may have custody of the country's sovereign wealth fund. Central banks usually oversee the commercial banks of their country. It issues the national currency, the nation's money. It controls the overall supply of money and monetary policy. In contrast to a commercial bank, a central bank can increase or decrease the amount of money in the nation.
